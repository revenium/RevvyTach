### Idea 2 (i-20261025-02) · Choose which accounts share my Claude conversations, with work seats kept apart

**Their problem today.** A developer links a work Team seat and two personal Claude accounts. The link dialog says "settings are shared." It never says every linked account now shares one pool of conversations and Claude's saved project memory. A work refactor shows up in a personal account's resume list; one more message sends employer code under personal terms. Nobody chose this.

**Their reality after.** Each account has a "Conversation history" setting: shared with my accounts, shared within this organization, or separate. Personal accounts keep today's sharing, so `claude --continue` still works after a switch; work seats default to separate. Settings shows each pool. Across a boundary, RevvyTach offers "Bring along: refactor, 4 min ago", asking first before work content lands in a personal account.

**Where you'd see it:** Settings → CLI Account, a "Conversation history" row; the Link Account dialog; the popover's Accounts list after Make Active.

**For example** (illustrative numbers)**.** "work-team" is separate; "personal-1" and "personal-2" share. personal-1 hits 100%, Make Active on personal-2 (31% used), and `--continue` resumes. Switching from work-team to personal-2 offers Bring along with a warning.

**As a** developer with several Claude accounts, **when** I link and switch accounts, **I want** to decide which ones share conversations **so that** work follows me only where it belongs.

**Target user:** developer with several Claude/Codex accounts · **Strategy:** "the terminal following via tmux and rc pointers … under a strict no-exfiltration … stance"; "put my terminal on it in one click".

**Already tracked:** nothing open. **What this adds:** history sharing becomes a choice, not a linking side effect.

**Why it matters.** Moving the terminal with you (JTBD-2) is only trustworthy when what moves is chosen. Today the app itself silently mixes work conversations into personal accounts.

**Cost.** Effort L. **Biggest risk.** Separating a pool that running `claude` sessions are writing to, without losing a conversation.
**An agent could build this unattended:** no.
**Score** 20 of 25 = job value 4 × strategic fit 5. Demand none. Risk 4, effort L.

Revvie could implement unattended: no. The cross-org rule and the migration of live folders are owner calls, and proof needs real signed-in accounts and a live `claude --continue`.

**Risk in full.**
- Migration: unsharing symlinked `projects/` and `sessions/` under running processes can lose or interleave writes; gate on no live process, swap the link atomically, keep the backup.
- The past cannot be cleaned: work transcripts already in the shared pool stay readable by personal accounts unless the user moves or deletes them; the setting protects from now on, and must say so.
- Continuity regression: a seat set to separate loses `--continue` across accounts; that is the point, but needs plain copy.
- Privacy: Bring along from work to personal sends employer content to Anthropic under personal terms on the next reply; always ask.
- Upstream dependence on Claude Code's documented, versioned folder layout; unknown layout hides the feature.
- Localization: roughly twelve strings × 9 locales.

**Alternatives.**
- Do nothing. Leaves an undisclosed cross-org pool created by the app.
- Disclosure only (fix the dialog and docs): cheap and should ship regardless, but gives no control.
- The original first slice (copy one conversation after every switch): mostly lands on its own "already shared" branch; kept only as Bring along across boundaries.
- A single global share toggle: cannot express "work separate, personal shared".
- Sibling i-20261025-14 (everything in `~/.claude` reaches every account) overlaps on the link symlinks; conversations need their own privacy rule.
