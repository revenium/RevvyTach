### Idea 4 (i-20261025-04) · Know when an account you switched away from is still using up its limit

**Their problem today.** Work-2 hit 88% of its 5-hour window, so you pressed Make Active on work-3. New terminals follow; open ones stay put. If a forgotten pane or your other Mac keeps using work-2, its bar creeps toward the wall unannounced. You must remember the 88% and spot 94%, or check every terminal by hand.

**Their reality after.** The account you left gets a quiet note only if its usage keeps rising: "work-2 +6% since you switched." Open it for the reason: "2 sessions on this Mac: api, web" or "used elsewhere." Nothing moves; a flat account shows nothing. Codex follows.

**Where you'd see it:** the health strip's account list (left-click the strip), on the left account's row; the popover's account chip gets a small mark.

**For example** (illustrative numbers)**.** At 10:00 you switch from work-2 (88%) to work-3. At 10:40 work-2 reads 94%; its row names "web", a forgotten pane. You close it before work-2 locks you out until 3pm.

**As a** developer with several Claude/Codex accounts, **when** I have switched away from an account near its limit, **I want** to be told if it is still being used, and from where, **so that** I don't burn the account I meant to rest.

**Target user:** developer with several Claude/Codex accounts · **Strategy:** the bet's "the terminal following via tmux and rc pointers" — this checks that it did.

**Already tracked:** nothing open. **What this adds:** a since-the-switch rise on the account you left, explained by its running sessions.

**Why it matters.** It serves "Move the terminal with me": today only a hand check tells you whether a switch took. It flags real spending, not idle panes.

**Cost.** Effort M (first slice S). **Biggest risk.** The session explanation miscounting, for example Claude Desktop's own copy of Claude Code.
**An agent could build this unattended:** no.
**Score** 20 of 25 = job value 4 × strategic fit 5. Demand none. Risk 3, effort M.
