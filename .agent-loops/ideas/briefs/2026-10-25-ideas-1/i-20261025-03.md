### Idea 3 (i-20261025-03) · When an account runs out, land on one with real room, not just the next in line

**Their problem today.** A developer with five Claude accounts hits the limit. Auto-switch and the Next Profile shortcut both take the next account in list order that is not completely full, so a 99% account wins and fails minutes later. Auto-switch also ignores a full weekly limit. A second account in one pane means hand-typing a long setting.

**Their reality after.** Auto-switch, and a new "Switch to Best Account" shortcut, skip nearly full accounts, then pick the one whose weekly allowance resets soonest, so expiring room gets used first. The Accounts list tags that account "Next up". Next Profile keeps list order. Each account's right-click menu gains "Copy Terminal Command" to run that account in one pane, active account untouched.

**Where you'd see it:** Settings → Shortcuts; a "Next up" tag in the popover's Accounts list; each account's right-click menu.

**For example** (illustrative numbers)**.** Work hits 100%. Side is at 97%; Personal 40%, weekly reset in 3 days; Spare 30%, resetting tonight. Today: Side. After: Spare.

**As a** developer with several Claude/Codex accounts, **when** an account runs out, **I want** the switch to land on an account with room **so that** I am not moved twice or waste expiring room.

**Target user:** developer with several Claude/Codex accounts · **Strategy:** "see at a glance which of my accounts still has headroom and put my terminal on it in one click"

**Already tracked:** nothing open; Idea 5 (weekly runway) proposes this order. **What this adds:** one destination rule behind every automatic switch.

**Why it matters.** At the limit (JTBD-1), the app's own switch can pick the worst account, worst of all in auto-switch, where nobody watches.

**Cost.** Effort S. **Biggest risk.** Setting the "nearly full" floor too high leaves auto-switch stuck.
**An agent could build this unattended:** yes, once the owner sets the floor.
**Score** 15 of 25 = job value 3 × strategic fit 5. Demand none. Risk 2, effort S.
