### Idea 7 (i-20261026-07) · Hear when an account stops working, and get one summary instead of a pile of alerts

**Their problem today.** When an account's Claude Code sign-in dies, only a small red menu bar dot shows it. You find out at a limit: you pick that account and the claude command demands a login. Alerts pile up: a desktop Mac sends an "auto-start" alert (a new session opened at reset) for each overnight reset, then a "session reset" alert per account at screen wake.

**Their reality after.** When an account becomes unusable, one notification names it, with a Fix button, so you repair it before you need it. Alerts that land together arrive as one summary. On a Mac that stayed awake, a "While you were away" banner lists what reset, what auto-start did, what broke, and the account with most room, with Make Active (points your terminal at it).

**Where you'd see it:** macOS notifications; a banner atop the RevvyTach popover.

**For example** (illustrative numbers)**.** Mac Studio, five accounts. Today: nine alerts by 07:55, and Side Max's dead sign-in found at 14:20 mid-limit. After: one 07:55 summary, "4 reset, best start Work Max [Make Active]"; at 10:30 "Side Max: sign-in expired [Fix]"; at 14:20 Side Max switches cleanly.

**As a** developer with several accounts, **when** a sign-in breaks, **I want** to hear at once, with other alerts summarized, **so that** every account is ready at my limit.

**Target user:** developer with several Claude/Codex accounts · **Strategy:** "put my terminal on it in one click — without browser tabs, re-logins"

**Already tracked:** nothing open. **What this adds:** an alert for a silent failure, and one summary instead of many alerts.

**Why it matters.** Serves trusting the app with my keys. A dead sign-in found at the limiter breaks the "no re-logins" promise.

**Cost.** Effort M. **Biggest risk.** A false "sign-in broken" alert right after wake, before the network is back.
**An agent could build this unattended:** yes.
**Score** 15 of 25 = job value 3 × strategic fit 5. Demand none. Risk 3, effort M.
