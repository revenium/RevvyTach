### Idea 8 (i-20261026-08) · Know which real account every profile is, and get flagged when one changes or overlaps another

**Their problem today.** A Claude profile is known only by the name you typed; RevvyTach never shows the email or plan behind it. Its terminal or browser sign-in can quietly become a different account, say a terminal sign-in finished in the wrong browser window; RevvyTach adopts it silently. It happened on the maintainer's Mac, noticed only when numbers looked wrong.

**Their reality after.** Each profile shows its real account: "jane@acme.com · Acme · Team seat." It is re-checked whenever a sign-in changes, terminal ones included. If a profile's account changes, its two sign-ins disagree, or two profiles are one account, it is marked until fixed or called intended. Codex gets the same duplicate check.

**Where you'd see it:** Settings → Manage Profiles under each name; the popover header; a mark on the menu bar item and Health strip bar; a receipt after signing in from the setup wizard or Settings → Claude Account.

**For example** (illustrative numbers)**.** You redo the terminal sign-in for "Personal 2" and get jane@gmail.com. A minute later it is marked: "Same account as Personal 1 (was jane.side@gmail.com)." You sign in again; the mark clears.

**As a** developer with several Claude accounts, **when** any sign-in changes, **I want** each profile's real account shown and overlaps flagged, **so that** no profile silently counts the wrong account.

**Target user:** developer with several Claude/Codex accounts; Team/Enterprise member · **Strategy:** "see at a glance which of my accounts still has headroom"

**Already tracked:** PRODUCT-3349 (a sign-in landing in the wrong profile; status unknown) fixes one cause. **What this adds:** a standing identity per profile, checked on every sign-in change.

**Why it matters.** Two profiles on one account show room you lack (job 1, find headroom); mismatched sign-ins misplace spend (job 5, trust the numbers).

**Cost.** Effort L. **Biggest risk.** Nagging people whose overlap is deliberate.
**An agent could build this unattended:** no.
**Score** 20 of 25 = job value 4 × strategic fit 5. Demand none. Risk 3, effort L.

**Risk in full.**
- Privacy: emails are shown locally only. Never transmitted; the app has no telemetry. Keep them out of logs, diagnostics and history exports (`SensitiveDataRedactor` and serialization-guard tests need a case).
- Upstream dependence: `oauth/profile` is undocumented. Missing fields show "account unknown", never a mark or a blocked save.
- Request volume: one extra lookup per new credential on terminal-only profiles; must keep the rotation rollforward or 429s return.
- Blast radius: a false mark on a deliberate setup; marks are acknowledgeable and never block.
- Localization: ~15 keys × 9 locales; long emails need truncation and fit tests (Japanese, German).
- Concurrency: identity rides the per-profile refresh actor; comparison needs a consistent snapshot.

**Alternatives.**
- Do nothing: rely on PRODUCT-3349. Closes one cause; the next misrouting door is silent again.
- Save-point receipt only (the original scope): misses the recorded terminal `/login` case.
- Identity display only, no marks: most "which account is this" value at half the cost, but nobody looks until numbers already look odd.
- Read Claude Code's local account record: no request, but an undocumented local file format.

**Strongest objection (critic).** OBJECTION-RESCOPES — The guard sits at the wrong door. It fires only at RevvyTach's own save points (wizard, Re-sync, Link), but the maintainer's incident (PRODUCT-3349) arrived through Claude Code's own `/login` in a terminal picking the wrong browser account, which RevvyTach then adopted passively from the linked config dir (CHANGELOG #111). No receipt fires on that path, so as scoped the idea misses its one recorded case. It also ignores the mismatch inside one profile: the terminal and browser sign-ins can be different accounts, putting one org's spend under another member (failure #1). Rescope: identity becomes a standing per-profile property, re-checked whenever the credential changes (the `oauth/profile` lookup already re-fires on each new credential fingerprint, `ClaudeAPIService.swift` `cliOrganizationID`), compared across both sign-ins and all profiles, and shown as a persistent mark on the strip and popover until resolved; the sign-in receipt is its first surface, not the whole. Job value 4 (save-point-only version is 3). Fit 5. Risk 3: undocumented email field, new personal data on screen. Effort L.

**Response.** Scope grew. Wrong-door objection held: the app adopts a fresh terminal sign-in passively (4.3.1 #113; #111 is the live-session renewal case, so the citation is slightly off) and checks no identity on it. Re-fire held with a limit: the lookup re-runs per new fingerprint, but only for profiles with a browser-sign-in org, so terminal-only profiles need a new once-per-credential call. "Nothing compares them" partly failed: an organization-level terminal-versus-browser check exists, but only shows a line in the extra-usage row, raises no mark by design, and two members of one Team org pass it. That design note is why I diverge on "persistent until resolved": a mark must be acknowledgeable, or a deliberate team-browser/personal-terminal setup carries a permanent complaint. Identity is now a standing property, re-checked on every credential change, compared within and across profiles; the receipt is a surface. Job value 4 stands (partial-coverage cap). Fit 5 stands. Risk 2→3, effort M→L accepted.
