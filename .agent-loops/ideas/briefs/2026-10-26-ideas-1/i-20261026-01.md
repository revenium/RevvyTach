### Idea 1 (i-20261026-01) · One shortcut shows every account's room and switches by number, even with the menu bar hidden

**Their problem today.** At a limit in a full-screen terminal, you mouse to the top edge, find RevvyTach (if a menu bar manager hasn't hidden it) and click accounts one by one: the popover's account list shows names, not room. The Health strip's list shows room, Claude only. With the icon hidden, the popover shortcut can open off screen or do nothing.

**Their reality after.** The shortcut always works. It opens the Health strip's account list, now with Codex too, centred when the icon is hidden. Numbered rows show session and weekly room, next reset, the active account and broken sign-ins. Press a number to make it active; hold the shortcut to peek. A limit alert opens it too. Nothing switches unless you ask.

**Where you'd see it:** the popover shortcut (Settings → Shortcuts), the Health strip's account list, and the limit alert.

**For example** (illustrative numbers)**.** "Work Max" hits its limit. You click the alert: row 4, "Personal Pro", shows 12% session and 40% weekly used; row 6 reads "sign-in expired". You press 4; your next Claude session uses Personal Pro.

**As a** developer with many accounts, **when** I hit a limit away from the menu bar, **I want** one shortcut showing every account's room, picked by number, **so that** I never hunt for the icon.

**Target user:** developer with several Claude/Codex accounts · **Strategy:** "see at a glance which of my accounts still has headroom and put my terminal on it in one click"

**Already tracked:** nothing open. **What this adds:** the existing list, by keyboard and alert, for both providers.

**Why it matters.** Finding headroom (job 1), moving the terminal (job 2) and looking without touching (job 3), when the menu bar is out of reach.

**Cost.** Effort M. **Biggest risk.** Number keys captured while open must always be released.
**An agent could build this unattended:** no.
**Score** 20 of 25 = job value 4 × strategic fit 5. Demand none. Risk 3, effort M.
