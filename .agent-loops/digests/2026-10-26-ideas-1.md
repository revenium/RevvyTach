### Idea 1 (i-20261026-01) · One shortcut shows every account's room and switches by number, even with the menu bar hidden

**Their problem today.** At a limit in a full-screen terminal, you mouse to the top edge, find RevvyTach (if a menu bar manager hasn't hidden it) and click accounts one by one: the popover's account list shows names, not room. The Health strip's list shows room, Claude only. With the icon hidden, the popover shortcut can open off screen or do nothing.

**Their reality after.** The shortcut always works. It opens the Health strip's account list, now with Codex too, centred when the icon is hidden. Numbered rows show session and weekly room, next reset, the active account and broken sign-ins. Press a number to make it active; hold the shortcut to peek. A limit alert opens it too. Nothing switches unless you ask.

**Where you'd see it:** the popover shortcut (Settings → Shortcuts), the Health strip's account list, and the limit alert.

**For example** (illustrative numbers)**.** "Work Max" hits its limit. You click the alert: row 4, "Personal Pro", shows 12% session and 40% weekly used; row 6 reads "sign-in expired". You press 4; your next Claude session uses Personal Pro.

**As a** developer with many accounts, **when** I hit a limit away from the menu bar, **I want** one shortcut showing every account's room, picked by number, **so that** I never hunt for the icon.

**Target user:** developer with several Claude/Codex accounts · **Strategy:** "see at a glance which of my accounts still has headroom and put my terminal on it in one click"

**Already tracked:** nothing open. **What this adds:** the existing list, by keyboard and alert, for both providers.

**Why it matters.** Finding headroom (job 1), moving the terminal (job 2) and looking without touching (job 3), when the menu bar is out of reach.

**Cost.** Effort M. **Biggest risk.** Number keys captured while open must always be released.

### Idea 3 (i-20261026-03) · Find the account that can still run Opus (or Sonnet) this week, and switch to it in one click

**Their problem today.** Claude stops you at the 5-hour session, the all-models weekly, or, on some plans, a weekly cap for one model. RevvyTach judges room from the first two only. **Their reality after.** You pick the model you need (default: all models). Every account row shows the room left for it and names the limit that binds. Rows sort by that room, Make Active on each. Auto-switch lands only where that model runs. A "Compare accounts" window shows every limit. A dash means no reading, never 0%.

**Where you'd see it:** a "Room for" picker atop the health strip's account list and the "+N" list; beside auto-switch in Settings; "Compare accounts" at both lists' foot.

**For example** (illustrative numbers)**.** Opus fills on "Work Max". Pick Opus: "Personal Max" 30% left (Opus cap); "Side Max" 8% (all-models weekly). Make Active on "Personal Max".

**Target user:** developer with several Claude/Codex accounts · **Strategy:** "I can see at a glance which of my accounts still has headroom and put my terminal on it in one click"

### Idea 4 (i-20261026-04) · Run separate kinds of work on separate accounts at the same time

**Their problem today.** One switch moves every new terminal on the Mac, so client work, personal work and an agent loop can't stay on separate accounts. Running a second account alongside means hand-typing a setup line. When a side account runs out, nothing notices: automatic switching watches only the active account.

**Their reality after.** They name a few "lanes", such as client and loop, each on its own account. A lane is a label a terminal carries, set on a tmux session or a terminal profile. Make Active works per lane. When a lane's account hits its limit, only that lane moves, onto an account no other lane uses. Unlabelled terminals follow today's switch. "Copy launch command" opens one terminal on any account without moving anything.

**Where you'd see it:** right-click any account in the menu bar or Health strip → "Copy launch command" and "Use for lane…"; a lane tag in the popover Accounts list; Settings → CLI Account → Lanes.

**For example** (illustrative numbers)**.** Eight accounts, three lanes. At 14:10 the loop's Max hits its limit; the loop lane moves to an idle Max. Personal and client never notice.

**As a** developer with several accounts, **when** I run separate kinds of work in parallel, **I want** each kept on its own account **so that** switching or running out in one never moves the others.

**Target user:** developer with several Claude/Codex accounts · **Strategy:** "put my terminal on it in one click"; "the terminal following via tmux and rc pointers"

**Already tracked:** nothing open. **What this adds:** named lanes, each with its own switch, plus a launch command for both providers.

**Why it matters.** It serves JTBD-2 (move the terminal with me) for accounts used in parallel.

**Cost.** Effort L. **Biggest risk.** Per-lane automatic switching changes shipped automation; a wrong move puts two lanes on one account.
**An agent could build this unattended:** no.
**Score** 20 of 25 = job value 4 × strategic fit 5. Demand none. Risk 4, effort L.
